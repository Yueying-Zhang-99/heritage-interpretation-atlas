# Atlas curation notes

The timeline is a chronological research index. It does not assert that heritage interpretation has three established theoretical streams. The previous A/B/C stream values remain in the JSON only for compatibility with older exports; the public interface does not use them.

Dot colour denotes document type. Dot size uses `importance` as a **provisional researcher code** for contribution to heritage interpretation: 1 = contextual reference, 2 = field-shaping framework, 3 = direct theoretical or interpretive shift. These levels are qualitative working judgments, not citation counts or measurements of objective impact. `importance_note` records a short rationale. `placeholder: true` produces a dashed ring and means close reading and analytical coding are pending.

Source excerpts are stored as short verbatim quotations in `excerpts`, with `source` and `location`. Only excerpts checked against accessible original or publisher texts have been added. A missing excerpt is displayed honestly as pending; summaries and previews are paraphrases, not quotations.

- The London Charter, version 2.1, February 2009, Principle 2: https://londoncharter.org/principles.html ; version history: https://londoncharter.org/history.html

- Milgram & Kishino, 1994, publisher abstract: https://globals.ieice.org/en_transactions/information/10.1587/e77-d_12_1321/_p

The UNESCO digital-heritage charter concerns preservation of digital resources. It should not be treated as a direct charter on interpreting physical historic places with AR/MR. The London Charter is more directly relevant to evidence, transparency and documentation in heritage visualisation.

The Delhi Declaration on Heritage and Democracy links heritage management with rights, cultural plurality, interpretation ethics, community participation and communication technologies. It is a policy-level framework, not a practical specification for MR or gamified interpretation. Its network links to interpretation and digital-visualisation documents are thematic comparisons, not claims of direct influence.

The Timeline plots publication year (or the explicitly labelled inscription/index date) on the horizontal axis. **Bands** retains a linear time axis and one `timeline_topic` as a primary row. **Flow** follows year order with explicitly labelled **uneven spacing** that gives dense years more horizontal room; horizontal distance in Flow does not measure elapsed time. It uses `topic_memberships`, an array of qualitative, nonexclusive research codes. A record is plotted once, and every assigned theme envelope passes through it. The vertical placement is a reading layout; its distance does not measure conceptual similarity. These four working themes are not established theoretical streams.

Each membership stores `topic`, `status`, `evidence`, `source_url` and `location`. `source` means an accessible original or official description has been checked at that location, not that the entire book has been reviewed. `provisional` marks researcher interpretations still awaiting close reading. A checked membership can coexist with a dashed record whose broader reading remains pending. Older imports without memberships fall back to their single Bands topic, labelled provisional. The editor supports multiple checkboxes and separate evidence for each theme. Cluster and Filters expose the same memberships.

Flow fits the current 35-record collection into the available viewport. It measures title/maker labels at their normal font sizes, puts the year below each dot, and uses reproducible collision avoidance across a shared canvas, with soft preferences for each record's primary `timeline_topic`. Full date/version labels and authors or organisations remain in the hover tooltip and reading card. Very narrow windows or larger imports can gain horizontal scrolling; unusually dense imports retain a readable internally scrollable map. Its envelopes include **all** assigned memberships. A continuous outer shell follows the upper and lower extent of coded members across dates, softened towards a padded hull to avoid sharp or deep notches. Nearby dates share rounded shoulders. Broad empty regions become enclosed internal cavities; a protected rim keeps these openings away from the outside edge. A minimum-length branching field protects the member dots and routes through shared records while selecting cavities. Gaussian smoothing and closed splines round both the outside and hole boundaries. Selected or hovered themes use a continuous border. The shell, shoulder grouping, cavity limits, field widths and smoothing are graphic layout choices, not historical findings. Envelopes change with filtering; their width, area, overlap and continuity do not quantify influence, importance or scholarly consensus, establish historical continuity or causation, or imply comprehensive coverage of the intervening years. A bridge can pass near an unrelated node; membership is determined by the coded theme list shown on hover and in the reading card, not containment alone. Hovered dotted links come only from existing `relations`; their type and evidence remain in the reading card. An envelope is not a genealogical connection. The reference is Charles Jencks’s chronograms, interpreted as a visual precedent rather than a validated heritage taxonomy: https://www.e-flux.com/architecture/chronograms

Version discipline: the Burra node is dated to its first adoption in 1979. Participation and interpretation provisions from the 2013 edition are not retroactively coded onto the 1979 node. UNESCO inscriptions used for practice cases do not date every intervention. Robben Island’s 2011 mission report includes shortcomings and unimplemented plans, not only successful practice. The accessible Tongariro case page supports community volunteer involvement; it does not substantiate the earlier claim of Māori co-authoring exhibitions, which has been corrected.

Policy additions and document forms (2026-10-03):

Ten reading-pending records have been added: Lausanne Charter (1990), Valletta Convention (1992), Cultural Tourism Charter (1999), UNESCO Intangible Heritage Convention (2003), Québec Declaration (2008), Burra Charter revision (2013), Australia ICOMOS Interpretation Practice Note (2013, version 1), Seville Principles (2017 endorsement version), Salalah Guidelines (2017), and the International Charter and Guidance on Sites with Intangible Cultural Heritage (2024). Each has an official source, a short verified excerpt, selected clause locations, working multi-theme codes and reading questions. This is a selective reading scaffold, not a complete review of each document. Relationships are thematic comparisons unless their evidence identifies an explicit reference or version history. Excerpts remain short; notes are paraphrases, not translations of the complete texts.

`document_nature` is an optional array of editable descriptive forms within the existing Charter / Policy display category: Convention, Charter, Declaration, Principles, Guidelines, Practice Note, Recommendation and Document. A combined charter and guidance may use two forms. This field classifies document form, not enforceability, jurisdiction or ratification status. Existing policy records inherit a form from their existing type; older user imports remain valid without this field. The field is available in detail cards, Library, Filters, Cluster and Chinese label management. New nodes retain dashed outlines until complete reading is finished; source-checked memberships refer only to their specified clauses.

Version discipline for these additions: the 2013 Burra Charter and Interpretation Practice Note are separate from the 1979 adoption node. The Seville node is dated to ICOMOS endorsement in 2017, not its initial formulation. The Valletta Convention node uses its 1992 signing date, not the 1995 entry into force. The intangible-sites charter uses its 2024 adoption, not the 2025 upload of the bilingual PDF. Chinese titles without a verified official translation are research working translations. The Salalah Guidelines explicitly describe themselves as suggestions rather than regulations or standards.
