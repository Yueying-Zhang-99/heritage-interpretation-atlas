# Atlas curation notes

The timeline is a chronological research index. It does not assert that heritage interpretation has three established theoretical streams. The previous A/B/C stream values remain in the JSON only for compatibility with older exports; the public interface does not use them.

Dot colour denotes document type. Dot size uses `importance` as a **provisional researcher code** for contribution to heritage interpretation: 1 = contextual reference, 2 = field-shaping framework, 3 = direct theoretical or interpretive shift. These levels are qualitative working judgments, not citation counts or measurements of objective impact. `importance_note` records a short rationale. `placeholder: true` produces a dashed ring and means close reading and analytical coding are pending.

Source excerpts are stored as short verbatim quotations in `excerpts`, with `source` and `location`. Only excerpts checked against accessible original or publisher texts have been added. A missing excerpt is displayed honestly as pending; summaries and previews are paraphrases, not quotations.

- The London Charter, version 2.1, February 2009, Principle 2: https://londoncharter.org/principles.html ; version history: https://londoncharter.org/history.html
- UNESCO Charter on the Preservation of Digital Heritage, 2003, Article 2: https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/charter-preservation-digital-heritage
- ICOMOS Delhi Declaration on Heritage and Democracy, 2017, sections 2–3: https://www.icomos.org/images/DOCUMENTS/Charters/GA2017_Delhi-Declaration_20180117_EN.pdf
- Milgram & Kishino, 1994, publisher abstract: https://globals.ieice.org/en_transactions/information/10.1587/e77-d_12_1321/_p

The UNESCO digital-heritage charter concerns preservation of digital resources. It should not be treated as a direct charter on interpreting physical historic places with AR/MR. The London Charter is more directly relevant to evidence, transparency and documentation in heritage visualisation.

The Delhi Declaration on Heritage and Democracy links heritage management with rights, cultural plurality, interpretation ethics, community participation and communication technologies. It is a policy-level framework, not a practical specification for MR or gamified interpretation. Its network links to interpretation and digital-visualisation documents are thematic comparisons, not claims of direct influence.

The Timeline plots publication year (or the explicitly labelled inscription/index date) on the horizontal axis. **Bands** retains one `timeline_topic` as a primary row and uses the same hybrid time-spacing rule as Flow. It jointly chooses left/right label directions and packs non-overlapping intervals to reduce the number of vertical tracks. Labels are measured at the existing font sizes. Band heights follow their required track count; remaining viewport space is distributed evenly. Short windows can expand the plot horizontally (up to five 10% steps) before adding vertical scroll, and the footer discloses this reading mode. **Flow** uses linear elapsed-time spacing through **1990**, so the 1930–1990 decade intervals are equal. After 1990 it uses explicitly labelled expanded, uneven spacing to give dense years more reading room; distances across that later segment do not measure elapsed time. The allocation between the two segments adapts to the available width while retaining the equal early intervals. It uses `topic_memberships`, an array of qualitative, nonexclusive research codes. A record is plotted once, and every assigned theme envelope passes through it. The vertical placement is a reading layout; its distance does not measure conceptual similarity. These five working themes are not established theoretical streams.

Each membership stores `topic`, `status`, `evidence`, `source_url` and `location`. `source` means an accessible original or official description has been checked at that location, not that the entire book has been reviewed. `provisional` marks researcher interpretations still awaiting close reading. A checked membership can coexist with a dashed record whose broader reading remains pending. Older imports without memberships fall back to their single Bands topic, labelled provisional. The editor supports multiple checkboxes and separate evidence for each theme. Cluster and Filters expose the same memberships.

Flow fits the current 36-record collection into the available viewport. It measures title/maker labels at their normal font sizes, puts the year below each dot, and uses reproducible collision avoidance across a shared canvas, with soft preferences for each record's primary `timeline_topic`. Full date/version labels and authors or organisations remain in the hover tooltip and reading card. Very narrow windows or larger imports can gain horizontal scrolling; unusually dense imports retain a readable internally scrollable map. Its envelopes include **all** assigned memberships. A continuous outer shell follows the upper and lower extent of coded members across dates, softened towards a padded hull to avoid sharp or deep notches. Nearby dates share rounded shoulders. Broad empty regions become enclosed internal cavities; a protected rim keeps these openings away from the outside edge. Rim thickness grows moderately with the canvas size. Each candidate cavity is eroded inward to retain at most about 62% of its candidate area, with a minimum inset, so large displays retain substantial connecting bands rather than thin tubes. A minimum-length branching field protects the member dots and routes through shared records while selecting cavities. Gaussian smoothing and closed splines round both the outside and hole boundaries. Selected or hovered themes use a continuous border. The shell, shoulder grouping, cavity limits, field widths and smoothing are graphic layout choices, not historical findings. Envelopes change with filtering; their width, area, overlap and continuity do not quantify influence, importance or scholarly consensus, establish historical continuity or causation, or imply comprehensive coverage of the intervening years. A bridge can pass near an unrelated node; membership is determined by the coded theme list shown on hover and in the reading card, not containment alone. Hovered dotted links come only from existing `relations`; their type and evidence remain in the reading card. An envelope is not a genealogical connection. The reference is Charles Jencks’s chronograms, interpreted as a visual precedent rather than a validated heritage taxonomy: https://www.e-flux.com/architecture/chronograms

Version discipline: the Burra node is dated to its first adoption in 1979. Participation and interpretation provisions from the 2013 edition are not retroactively coded onto the 1979 node. UNESCO inscriptions used for practice cases do not date every intervention. Robben Island’s 2011 mission report includes shortcomings and unimplemented plans, not only successful practice. The accessible Tongariro case page supports community volunteer involvement; it does not substantiate the earlier claim of Māori co-authoring exhibitions, which has been corrected.

`document_nature` is an optional array of editable descriptive forms within the existing Charter / Policy display category: Convention, Charter, Declaration, Principles, Guidelines, Practice Note, Recommendation and Document. A combined charter and guidance may use two forms. This field classifies document form, not enforceability, jurisdiction or ratification status. Existing policy records inherit a form from their existing type; older user imports remain valid without this field. The field is available in detail cards, Library, Filters, Cluster and Chinese label management. New nodes retain dashed outlines until complete reading is finished; source-checked memberships refer only to their specified clauses.

## Visitation and interpretive setting (2026-10-03)

The atlas now has five nonexclusive working themes. `Heritage visitation & tourism` concerns physical visits, visitor experience, routes, access, visitor pressures and responsible heritage tourism. It is an analytical theme, not a second medium category or an established disciplinary genealogy. Related records retain their existing themes. Tourism charters (1999/2022), The Tourist Gaze, existing practice cases and Salalah Guidelines use it as their primary Bands row; source-checked and provisional memberships are distinguished.

`interpretive_setting` is an editable array of descriptive labels independent of `media`: `On-site`, `Off-site`, `Both`, and `Unspecified / pending review`. Labels record the scope checked so far, not all exclusive applicability of a document. `Both` is included when filtering either On-site or Off-site; Cluster retains the explicit Both group. Older imports without the field appear as unspecified. The field is available in Filters, Cluster, Library, detail cards and Chinese content/tag management. `interpretive_setting_evidence` stores status, evidence, source URL and locator. Evidence can be source-checked even when the conclusion is that a general document does not specify a setting. Broad reading-pending technology papers remain unspecified. The London Charter is not coded off-site merely because it concerns digital visualisation.

The New Philadelphia AR tour is a reading-pending practice node supported by an official NPS activity description, not claimed to hold UNESCO/ICOMOS accreditation. The NPS page describes 11 on-site trail stops with digital buildings/people over the real landscape and narrated history. Its year 2025 is a **source-page update anchor** (31 March 2025), not the unverified AR project launch year; this distinction appears in `year_label`, `practice_date`, summary notes and source-excerpt locator. The node is unscored and retains the subdued practice styling. Its links are thematic comparisons, not documented direct influences. AR/MR can connect physical and digital interpretation when their design is situated; technology alone does not establish location or effectiveness.

This reading order does not assert an origin date or a causal sequence. `tools/check-flow-shell.cjs` verifies the shell topology and hybrid time scale.
